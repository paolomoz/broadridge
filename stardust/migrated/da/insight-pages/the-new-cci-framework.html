The New CCI Framework: Driving Better Disclosures and a Stronger U.K. Retail Investment Culture

U.K. households continue to hold relatively little in investments compared to global peers, largely due to lower financial literacy levels and complex, inaccessible investor disclosures. Regulators, fund managers, and distributors all agree on the need for change: without clearer, more transparent information on investment costs, risks, and performance, retail investors will remain disengaged and underserved.  

This feature article explores how the Financial Conduct Authority’s (FCA) new Consumer Composite Investments (CCI) framework aims to address this challenge. Drawing on Broadridge’s direct participation in FCA workshops, we examine the barriers to building a robust U.K. retail investment culture, from poor readability of Key Information Documents to ineffective data presentation. We also highlight practical, modern solutions such as layering disclosures, applying data visualisation to fund performance, and simplifying how risks and costs are conveyed to everyday investors.  

Because disclosure reform isn’t just a compliance exercise, it’s a critical opportunity to empower retail investors, strengthen trust in financial services, and encourage U.K. households to participate more confidently in investment markets.  

Read the feature article to learn how better disclosures can reshape investor engagement, reduce compliance risks for fund providers, and build a healthier U.K. investment culture.
