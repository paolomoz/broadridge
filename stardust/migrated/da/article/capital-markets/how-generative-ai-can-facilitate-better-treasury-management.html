Can we chat? How generative AI can facilitate better treasury management

Originally featured in Securities Finance Times

Treasury group: “What is the current client concentration of all third-party SFT liabilities? I need this broken down from highest to lowest.”

Repo trader: “I need to get back to you.”

Is that a familiar scenario? For the treasury group at firms that are active in the repo market, this kind of exchange is all too common – and often frustrating.

On the other side, repo traders are frequently interrupted by important yet poorly timed questions, which can be especially disruptive during periods of market volatility.

Now balance the repo desk’s need to optimize trade execution against the treasury group’s need for fast and constant feedback on leverage, liquidity and funding ratios. That information directly impacts the firm’s balance sheet and helps it to manage risk. And the market volatility that’s keeping repo traders busy may have triggered an early warning indicator, raising questions about market liquidity that the firm’s treasury group need immediate answers to.

Opportunities to drive efficiency with generative and agentic AI

In the future, artificial intelligence (AI) will offer an alternative to these inefficient interactions by facilitating data access.

AI isn’t the capital market’s first technology disruptor. Innovations such as trading algorithms, straight-through processing (STP) and distributed ledger technology (DLT) have transformed global markets. Technology has made it possible for new financial products, such as cryptocurrency, to be developed.

Today, capital market participants are adopting new AI techniques such as reinforcement learning, deep learning and natural language processing. These techniques help leverage insights for forecasting, trading and other operational efficiency and revenue-generating opportunities. For example, AI algorithms allow market participants to process unstructured data, which can help improve their trading strategies and risk management efforts. AI is freeing individuals from mundane tasks, such as responding to routine, often time-sensitive, questions.

Also emerging are agentic AI architectures, built around specialized, autonomous agents that can self-direct, collaborate, coordinate tasks, and independently execute workflows, transforming manual business processes into automated, intelligent operations.

Like many financial markets, the repo market, a primarily over-the-counter market, has taken a cautious approach to embracing digital transformation and integrating AI into its operations. This could be indicative of firms' reluctance to put their scarce resources to work until they see greater market acceptance via the existing infrastructure. 

Regulatory catalysts: T+1 and mandatory clearing

The repo market is in the process of moving away from voice trading and manual processing.

Two relatively recent regulatory changes, T+1 and mandated central counterparty clearing, have motivated market participants to make significant investment in technology for their repo trading and post-trade settlement systems.

In May 2024, a new T+1 settlement cycle deadline went into effect. Broker-dealers now have the trade day plus one additional day to settle transactions rather than the trade day and two additional days. With this tighter settlement deadline, firms had no choice but to begin using STP in their repo transactions.

An upcoming change that will test the repo market’s technology infrastructure is the move to mandated central counterparty clearing as of June 30, 2027. Firms will need to ensure their technology infrastructure is capable of scaling and integrating with new counterparties and central clearing agencies. With sponsored repo trade volume anticipated to grow, even dealers currently clearing trades through the Fixed Income Clearing Corp (FICC) will need to review — and possibly upgrade — their existing tech stack.

As of March 2025, the FICC is already clearing 46% of covered Treasury repo.[1] That compares to a reported 30% in 2024.

Need evidence?

The development of intraday repo — sometimes referred to as repo by the minute — provides an example of how technology can create positive changes in a financial market to the benefit of all participants.

As pointed out in an earlier article, until fairly recently, intraday repo was almost exclusively restricted to certain forms of central bank repo. That was because traditional repo market infrastructure wasn’t designed to support trades that were completed and settled in a matter of hours, sometimes minutes.

Now DLT provides the velocity, transparency and security needed to make intraday repo possible. As other banks and buy-side firms explore extending their repo businesses to intraday trading, DLT solutions appear poised to transform the repo market.

Looking ahead to 2027, DLT will play a pivotal role in helping dealers transition repo trade settlement to central clearing.

Building a better chatbot

Previously, chatbots and digital assistants relied on conversational AI. However, the 2022 launch of Open AI's chat generative pre-trained transformer (ChatGPT) changed how the world looks at — and uses —chatbots.

ChatGPT uses generative, rather than conversational, AI. This is an important distinction as generative AI is better equipped to process complex data. And unlike conversational AI, it can create new content. Generative AI can also be “trained” to automate repetitive tasks. The result: chatbots built with generative AI can perform research, generate reports and retrieve and organize data.

With its more sophisticated capabilities, generative AI is well suited to create tools that increase operational efficiencies for financial services firms’ institutional businesses. An example is Broadridge’s award-winning generative AI application for the corporate bond market, BondGPT , first launched in the LTX e-trading platform in 2023. Broadridge’s OpsGPT is another example, which helps manage operations across the post-trade lifecycle. Recent agentic AI enhancements to OpsGPT include automation of settlement fails analysis, optimized inventory management, and streamlined email communication workflows.

Generative AI and Agentic AI for treasury and repo insights

When it comes to solving the inefficient dynamics between the repo desk and treasury group, generative AI could be used to create efficiencies and save time. Chatbots and dashboards that proactively update information needed by the treasury group to better understand various points of liquidity and liquidity trends could minimize the need for back and forth and facilitate more seamless workflows.

For example, a treasury group asks for information that includes backdated data. To calculate the answer, a trader may need to step away from the repo desk for several hours during the trading day. In contrast, AI can provide this information to the treasury group faster and without interrupting the trade flow.

In addition to generative AI use cases, agentic AI can be applied to benefit treasury management. For example, it can be used for limit monitoring and alerting: if a limit threshold is breached, an AI agent can automatically send a warning email to notify relevant stakeholders.

For repo traders, spending less time during the trading day providing the treasury group with real-time input isn’t the only benefit of generative AI. Repo traders rely heavily on proprietary networks and third-party data providers that offer varying degrees of accuracy and timeliness. These fragmented data sources and the time-consuming data collecting process make it challenging for traders to make informed decisions on risk and ratios. AI’s ability to gather and aggregate data accurately and quickly would enhance operational efficiencies and provide repo traders with decision-making support.

The timing’s right

New market developments and regulatory changes make this an ideal time for repo market participants to look for ways to integrate AI to solve daily operational challenges. We are excited about the potential that generative AI and agentic AI offer our repo market clients — traders and their treasury group colleagues — as well as those in the broader securities finance arena and look forward to helping them unlock its potential.
